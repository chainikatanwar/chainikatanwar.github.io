01 · Regulatory technology

EU DAC7: building a compliance platform from zero

Project owner and technical lead · July to 31 January filing · 6–7 engineers, peaking at 10

A new EU directive required Agoda to collect, verify and report seller identity and income data across all 27 EU member states. There was no seller-data collection service, the existing reporting path was not PII-safe, and the first host submissions had to be live by October to leave time for the January filing. I led the rollout from requirements and system design through delivery and production response.

27EU member states covered by one filing pipeline
~97Kproperties with payouts blocked pending compliance; self-service unblocking on submission
$27Mestimated penalties avoided, per Tax team estimate
~3 monthskickoff to host data-collection launch

Problem and users

  • Hosts needed a clear way to submit country-specific tax identifiers without repeating data across properties.
  • Tax and Legal needed complete, accurate data and a reproducible filing by a fixed deadline.
  • Hotel Support needed guidance for identifier confusion and payout-blocking complaints.
  • Data needed a protected reporting path because the existing invoice-reporting tables were not PII-safe.

My ownership and boundaries

I owned the technical rollout, system design and delivery, split the work into collection and reporting tracks, wrote design documents and RFCs, and aligned Engineering, Legal and Data. Product and Legal retained policy and scope decisions; Tax selected the filing vendor after I raised the delivery risk of building the authorities’ SOAP integration in time.

Decisions and trade-offs

DecisionOptions consideredChosen approach and rationaleOwnership
Data scopeCollect broadly for future use vs. legal minimumCollect only legally required fields, with separate individual/entity paths: lower host friction and less PII to protect.Me with Legal
TIN validationBuild country rules vs. use EU validation APIUse the EU API for accuracy, with country-format rules as fallback so API degradation would not block submissions.Me
Reporting pathReuse existing tax-invoice pipeline vs. separate pathBuild an encrypted, access-restricted path because the existing tables were not PII-safe.Me with Data
Filing interfaceBuild SOAP integration vs. use filing vendorI flagged the schedule risk early; Tax sourced a vendor, decoupling our pipeline from the authorities’ interface.Risk call: me; vendor choice: Tax

Production learning shaped the product

  • Hosts confused TINs and VAT IDs; support-ticket themes led me to create a playbook and retain country-specific inline validation.
  • Blocking payouts for about 97K properties created a support spike; automatic unblocking removed the manual queue for compliant hosts.
  • A country-level Metabase dashboard tracked submission status across about one million in-scope hotels and informed warnings and enforcement.

Outcome and evidence

  • First DAC7 report filed by 31 January across all 27 markets.
  • Encrypted, access-restricted seller tax data path and versioned reporting snapshots.
  • Support playbook and self-service payout restoration.
  • $27M is an estimate supplied by the Tax team, not a directly measured cash saving.

Product judgment demonstrated

For deadline-driven compliance products, scope control, data minimisation, support readiness and a reliable fallback path are part of the product—not post-launch clean-up.

02 · Internal platform product

Central Tax Rules Service: moving tax logic out of the search release cycle

System designer and owner; implemented parts · 5 engineers · Tax, Search and Product stakeholders

British Columbia required different tax rates across about 100 cities, plus exemptions based on hotel chain and customer origin. Rules were hard-coded inside a Search-owned engine, so each change took about 10 days and left no audit trail. I designed a Tax-owned rules platform and negotiated an integration model that addressed Search’s latency and refactoring concerns.

~10 days → ~1 mintime to change a tax rule or rate
5jurisdictions onboarded on the shared platform
~100cities with different rates in the first launch
0 addedsearch latency from the embedded client library

Problem and users

  • Tax needed to change rules without depending on Search code releases.
  • Search needed to protect request latency and avoid owning tax logic.
  • Product and compliance needed correct tax treatment by each regulatory date.
  • Travelers and accommodation partners needed accurate taxes in search and booking.

My ownership

I traced the existing search-side filtering path, designed the service and client-library approach, and took the proposal through review with Search. I owned the platform design and implemented parts; the team delivered the rest. The final integration was agreed with Search and Tax.

Key product and architecture decisions

DecisionOptions consideredChosen approach and rationale
Platform vs. one-off rulesKeep hard-coding each jurisdiction vs. centralize rulesGeneric data-driven criteria, because the same pattern would recur across jurisdictions and annual rate changes.
GovernanceKeep SQL-updated cache vs. new APIAuthenticated write API with audit fields; the existing cache could not provide access control or reliable change history.
Search read pathLive service call per request vs. tax logic in Search vs. embedded client libraryTax-owned library embedded in Search, using an in-process cache and existing integration path. It met the latency constraint while shifting tax ownership to Tax.
Freshness and availabilityInstant push vs. bounded stalenessService refresh within 5 minutes and a 24-hour propagation SLA across Search pods, with last-known-good rules on refresh failure.

How alignment was achieved

Search was reluctant to add a live dependency or refactor its request path. I framed the proposal around its goals: remove tax change requests from Search, reuse an existing integration path and avoid per-request network calls. We reviewed each integration step together and tested latency in QA before launch.

Guardrails and production feedback

  • Only authenticated Tax engineers could update rules; each record captured who changed it and when.
  • Per-country dashboards compared expected and actual tax application. They caught a wrong percentage after Saudi Arabia onboarding.
  • QA previews and screenshots were reviewed before merge.
  • Warm cache and last-known-good rules protected Search during service or refresh failures.

Product judgment demonstrated

A platform proposal succeeds when it solves the recurring business problem and makes adoption rational for the team that must integrate it. The latency and ownership concerns were design inputs, not objections to push past.

03 · Platform security and adoption

Invoice System Authorization: securing a shared platform without interrupting issuance

Initiated and led design, implementation and rollout · 6–7 engineers · 12 consuming teams

After incorrect invoices were issued and the caller could not be traced, I proposed authorization for a shared invoice platform with roughly 30 open production and QA endpoints. The challenge was not only selecting an authorization mechanism: every consuming team had to migrate, including teams with unclear ownership, while critical invoices continued to flow.

12 / 12consuming teams moved to authenticated calls
~30endpoints protected
~25invoice types inventoried and covered
1 quarterpost-rollout invoice-volume observation

Problem and users

  • Invoice platform team needed caller identity and traceability.
  • 12 consumer teams needed a migration path that would not break scheduled invoices.
  • Hotel partners needed correct invoices on time.
  • Operations needed notice and a clear response plan during cutover.

My ownership

I proposed OPA, assessed the impact on the platform and callers, implemented the integration, and led the phased rollout. The OPA platform itself was maintained by a separate team; our team owned the invoice-system integration.

Decisions and trade-offs

DecisionOptions consideredChosen approach and rationale
Authorization mechanismBuild in-house vs. Okta vs. Open Policy AgentOPA, aligned with Agoda’s internal-service direction; Okta was a better fit for user-facing access, and custom auth would duplicate platform capability.
Rollout sequenceSwitch all callers at once vs. phase by riskStart with our own lowest-volume invoices, then move other teams from least to most critical to limit blast radius.
Critical invoice cutoverNormal operations vs. dedicated windowOne-week window beginning the day before scheduled issuance, with Operations briefed and the team on alert.
Orphaned invoice typesLeave unauthenticated vs. find owners vs. retireEscalate ownership through former team members and managers; retire the one or two types with no owner and no ongoing need.
RecoveryOne-way switch vs. reversible controlConsole flag to disable authorization without stopping invoice issuance.

Cross-team adoption

  • Mapped 12 teams, about 25 invoice types and about 30 endpoints.
  • Gave each team about a quarter to onboard and a named engineer as a point of contact.
  • Split invoice types across 6–7 engineers so every consuming team had direct support.
  • Worked with managers to resolve ownership gaps; unused orphaned invoice types were retired.

Rollout controls and outcome

  • QA verification before production traffic moved.
  • Invoice volumes compared with the same period before each cutover; CPU, memory, instance counts and response performance watched.
  • Rollback flag preserved invoice issuance if authorization caused an issue.
  • Critical invoices were sent after issues were recovered quickly; a full quarter of volume monitoring showed normal issuance.

→

The OPA platform is maintained by a separate team. The invoice team implemented the integration. A console flag provided a reversible recovery path during rollout.

Product judgment demonstrated

For a shared platform, the adoption plan is part of the product. Named ownership, a migration window, consumer support, observability and rollback made a security requirement operationally safe across 12 teams.
